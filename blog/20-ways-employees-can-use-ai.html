20 Ways Employees Can Use AI at Work

Not every AI use case is flashy — most of the real time savings come from small, everyday tasks.

AI for Business · 7 min read · Updated July 2026

Most conversations about AI in the workplace jump straight to dramatic use cases. In practice, the everyday time savings tend to come from smaller, less glamorous tasks — the kind every employee does every week. Here are twenty realistic starting points, organized by the type of work they support.

Writing & communication

  1. Drafting a first pass of routine emails, then editing for tone and accuracy
  2. Summarizing long email threads before a meeting
  3. Turning rough meeting notes into a clean, organized recap
  4. Drafting job descriptions or internal policy documents for review
  5. Rewriting dense technical content for a non-technical audience

Research & analysis

  1. Summarizing a long report or article down to the key points
  2. Comparing options (vendors, tools, approaches) side by side before a decision
  3. Explaining an unfamiliar term or concept quickly, mid-task
  4. Drafting an outline for a proposal or presentation before filling in the details

Spreadsheets & data

  1. Writing a spreadsheet formula from a plain-language description
  2. Explaining what an existing formula or macro actually does
  3. Spotting patterns or outliers in a dataset worth a second look
  4. Turning raw data into a first-draft chart or summary table

Customer & sales support

  1. Drafting responses to common customer questions for a human to review and send
  2. Summarizing customer feedback themes across many reviews or tickets
  3. Personalizing a sales outreach template without starting from scratch each time

Internal operations

  1. Creating a first-draft SOP (standard operating procedure) for a recurring task
  2. Drafting onboarding materials for a new hire
  3. Translating documents for non-English-speaking staff or customers
  4. Brainstorming a first list of options before a team discussion — names, approaches, ideas

The common thread

Nearly all of these are "first draft, then human review" use cases — AI does the blank-page work, a person checks it before it goes anywhere important. That pattern captures most of the real productivity gain without introducing the risk of AI-generated content going out unchecked.
